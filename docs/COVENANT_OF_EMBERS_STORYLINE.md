# Covenant of Embers Storyline

The campaign as it stands through **v0.9.0**, plus the approved outlines that are not built yet, plus a little connective tissue marked **Proposed** so ShobieShy can throw it out.

The Magewar storyline is the ancestor: Elyria, the Summoned, Crazy Joe, Bob, the landfill, the Filth, the Demon Lord. Its later chapters were blank. Covenant filled them with one labyrinth instead of five dungeons. This document is that filled story. The older file is not in this repository.

Read the tags the way the bible does. Unmarked scenes are in the game, or ShobieShy has already decided them. **Open** means you cannot play it yet, or nobody has answered it. **Proposed** means this document invented it.

## Before the rite

Elyria had dungeons the way a coast has weather. They were underground, mean, and contained. Heroes went in. Most of them came out. Crazy Joe is old enough to remember when that was the whole arrangement. The Magewar storyline gives him three centuries and one good story, a volcano dungeon sealed during the Eruption Wars, and then fifty years of getting worse.

Ten years ago the containers broke. The day is called the Fracture. Goblins, trolls, wraiths, and things with no names came up into farmland and onto roads. Cities that could pay for it, and mages who could survive the work, raised barriers. Eldoria got one. Merchants still argue under it. The dome holds. Joe will tell you, in the first five minutes, that the barriers are failing anyway.

Scholars spent the decade blaming a curse, a botched rite, or a ley line that tore. They can keep the argument. The Demon Lord caused the Breach. That is the decision of 11 Nov 2026. Eldoria has not heard it. Bob has half of it: after you survive the Filth, he will say the Fracture was deliberate, and he will not give you the name.

**Proposed.** He has the name. He is too lazy, and too afraid of being right, to spend it on you before you have earned a worse conversation.

The wilds outside the domes stayed wild. The Magewar storyline draws them as kingdoms and wastes and a sea. Covenant folds the parts you will actually meet into the shaft under the crater: Willowbrook's goblin problem, a wood that should not be underground, Ironforge's mines, a drowned temple, a sky where a sky cannot be. **Open** how much of that outer map is still there for a later game.

## The summoning

Joe does it in the Association hall, on the dais, with books he should not have had and mana the Elders will miss. He is mumbling about interdimensional echoes and forgotten gods when the smoke clears and you are standing in it.

He is delighted. You are a real outsider. A real chance. Then he puts you in front of the appraisal crystal, and the crystal says underwhelming.

He refuses the reading. He tells you the dungeons fractured ten years ago, that monsters are everywhere, that the barriers are failing, and that you should check the landfill for whatever is causing "it." The Elders arrive in the middle of the pitch. Joseph is expelled. Reckless does not begin to cover it. They march him out the back. The Summoned stay. The rite does not reverse. See the Clerk.

If you are alone, the other three are in the hall with you, having the same bad day. The Nerd expected a cheat skill. The Gal was at brunch. The Buff benches more than the crystal believes. The Loli is older than she looks and does not want the follow-up question. You pick which of them you are, and a name, and a color. The perk is small. The humiliation is shared.

**Proposed.** The crystal is not broken. The Demon Lord has been sitting on the summoning for years, spoiling Bob's quieter attempts and, this once, failing to stop Joe's loud one. What gets through is ordinary. Ordinary, in a realm that expected a champion, still counts as a crack in his plan. Joe does not understand this. Bob will, later, and he will pretend it is a research note.

The Clerk has Form 7-B. Sign or make a mark. Fifty gold, which will feed you for a week if Eldoria's prices are feeling kind. Clothes, enchanted for minor durability, already on your back. A residency permit for the Old Oak at the edge of town. Big dead tree. Can't miss it. The Clerk is already looking past you at the next problem.

**Open.** The permit, the quests, and the level all say Old Oak. On 11 Nov 2026 ShobieShy called Bob's home the Great Oak. This storyline uses Great Oak for the place and Old Oak for the paper. **Proposed.** Bob likes the grander name. The Clerk copied a survey.

## Eldoria, and a tree with a tenant

The square is paved and watched. Ward-pillars hold the dome. The Bored Guard knows where the landfill is and does not think the DANGER sign is decoration. Summoned show up once a decade, he says, and most of them do not last a dungeon. The Potion Merchant is proud of having the only potions in town and apologizes by insulting you. The Tailor can beat the Association's hand-me-downs if you have gold up front. The Runesmith wants parts and Arcane Dust and promises different luck, not a better rarity. In the Fractured Flagon, the ale tastes like barrier dust, and a patron who is already drunk will tell you the outer dungeons started acting up again last month.

You are a joke with a permit. The joke walks east.

The tree is hollow, dark, and candle-lit. Moss will do for a bed. Someone has been living here. That someone arrives, or is simply there, and he is not happy to see squatters. His name is Bob. He looks at you with a glowing orb. He is interested, which for Bob is a kind of affection he would deny.

You are weak. Pathetically so. You could become something useful. His only lead is a faint mana distortion at the landfill. He wants samples, and he wants Joe's trash, because Joe has a knack for accidental magic in his discards. The payment is a staff, a rusty dagger, a mana potion that tastes like sour berries, and a map on bark. The staff is for fighting. The dagger glows when something nasty is close, which in this town is a calendar. If you ask why you should help, he says the Association will not, and you have nowhere else to sleep. If you just agree, he likes you better. Less talking. More landfill.

The Association's back door opens on the dump. Joe has been throwing things through it for years. Tell the clerk Bob sent you. The Clerk, who did not enjoy the first conversation, waves you behind the dais. Mind the smell.

Bob has been in this tree for decades, hiding it, hating bureaucracy, and failing to summon anyone who could walk the Fracture for him. Those failures were the Demon Lord blocking the call. You are Joe's mess, sleeping in Bob's house, and you are the first result either of them has had in years. He will not say it that warmly.

## The Filth

The landfill is a tutorial in the sense that it tries to kill you with the smallest things first. The air is a green-brown haze. The sun looks sick. Gnawers nest in the trench and rush you in packs. Heaps of scrap stand up, slow, and slam. A wounded scavenger crawled out of a heap and did not get far. If you have Light, you can get them on their feet. They will point past the mound, toward the breach, and tell you not to go in bleeding. Those lines are still a placeholder voice. The person is real enough to bleed.

Joe is camped in his own garbage, delighted that the Association threw you out too. He calls it the real Association. He presses a wind wand on you, something that fell out of a portal last week, and he shows you a bench. Parts go in the staff. The rats bite. The parts do not. Under the mound, he says, something filthy lives.

The Filth is that something: a boss made of runoff, dungeon-slime, and years of Joe's discards. It slams like a heap that learned acid. It pulls the trash back into itself. The fight ends when the core is finally a target instead of a rumor. It always leaves Joe's trash for the quest. Sometimes it leaves a cinder that still remembers a buried fire, or a lens the Filth used to look through.

You can die here. The run is built to let you. See the bible for the last stand, and for the decided rule that a real death should dump you at the Association minus your gold. The landfill itself is a soft fail: the lesson is the staff, the element, and the walk back, not a game over.

## What Bob does with the sludge

You bring the trash home. Bob is surprised you are competent. Joe's madness has uses. The crystals in the junk still hold resonance, and out of them he makes a fast-travel crystal, a palm-sized way to jump between attuned stones. Then he says the sentence the whole campaign hangs on. The Fracture is not random. Someone, or something, caused it deliberately. Welcome to Elyria. Try not to break anything he cannot fix.

He also says the answers are in five dungeons scattered across the realm, and that you should talk to townsfolk and take their quests, and that he will be here researching, so stop bothering him. The five dungeons are the old shape of the truth. The Breach fused them. You will not tour Willowbrook, the woods, Ironforge, the sea, and the peaks as separate countries. You will fall through what they became.

The square changes its mind about you by an inch. The guard saw the sludge. The merchant offers first pick. The patron raises a mug and does not offer to pay for it. You are still a Summoned. You are no longer only a punchline.

## The Breach

North of the mound the road ends in a crater. The haze goes violet-grey. The rooms are a gauntlet: a gnawer den, a gallery of ember scorchlings, a cistern of ooze that splits when it dies, a switch, an arena. The Breach Warden is the vault's door, a dark heap that gets faster when it is hurt. When it dies, the labyrinth gate is a fact.

This is a half-level on purpose. The campaign's dungeon is the shaft, not the crater. Acts I through V are paths down that shaft. **Open** in the building sense: v0.9.0 still hands you the later acts as their own stretches after each boss, rather than one continuous walk from the King's hall to the nave. The story is one descent anyway.

## Act I — The Goblin Warrens

Packed earth, stolen timber, banners, spike traps, gnawers in the dark. These dens used to spill raiders toward Willowbrook. The deeper you go, the less it looks like a kingdom. The goblins are fleeing. Their crude wards against "green fire" mark the tunnels that lead down.

Bob's notes and the Association's scouts agree on a war-band and a crowned brute who taxes every raid. His throne room is bone piles and stolen Association crates. He slams, he throws, he roars, and the last of the pack comes when he calls. The crown hits the floor. A waystone lights for the square. The Fracture is not sealed. Roots and fungus are coming up through the lowest boards. Joe has started writing on the walls, in chalk and in blood, and the writing points the same way the wards do. You are holding a goblin key-token. You can go home. The hole will still be there.

## Act II — The Blightcoil

Under the warrens the shaft becomes a jungle. One cavern spirals around the Rotheart, a world-tree that had no business growing here and is dying of the Breach. The Sump is poison mire at the bottom. The Canopy is roots and moss and the last clean breath. The Crown is spore fog.

The goblins who ran this far are husks with spore-caps. Mossbound treants strangle what the husks miss. Joe came through first. His marks are cut into the bark. He has been feeding the Rotheart Breach-fire to speed the rot. The Verdant Mother is bound to the tree. She does not give a speech. The infection is climbing. Burn it out before it climbs higher.

Three of Joe's marks are enough to prove the sabotage. Then the Crown, and the Blightmaw Matriarch, a spore beast fused to the tree's heart. She fights you in the open, in three waves of worse: spores and husks, then a treant and the Crown's own vines, then a fog that makes the weak point matter. There is no door that locks you in. There is the tree, and her.

When she falls, the Rotheart dies. The fog clears. A door opens toward the mines. Under her is a cocoon, torn from the inside, empty. The Mother says she does not know what tore it open. She is telling the truth. Do not correct her.

## Act III — The Slagworks

The door drops you into mines that Association charts filed as a collapse. Rails, ore, lava, air that shimmers. The collapse was Joe. He flooded the lower shafts with Breach-fire. The furnaces never went cold.

Heat pushes you up onto gantries. Carts and constructs still run a schedule nobody is paid to keep. Every floor knows the furnace-anvil, a molten mouth you can orient by. The "workers" are branded trolls and forge-constructs, hammering Breach-ore into weapons for something deeper. A scrap-spirit on the overlook has the only name that matters. Forgemaw kept the fires lit. You are uninvited ore.

Joe's marks have changed material. They are slag seals burned into the rails. Three of them tell the same story as the bark: he was here, and he was not sightseeing.

Forgemaw's floor is a crucible. The gantry ring gives you flanks until he starts taking the ring away. Lava widens. A weak point opens because the cover is gone. When he dies, the main lift unlocks and the waystone home lights. At the bottom of the shaft, cooling slag has cast a perfect negative of a humanoid figure. The figure is gone. The scrap-spirit will tell you the shape is empty. The spirit will not tell you whose.

This is the third time the shaft has shown you a vacancy, if you count the cocoon. It is the second object. The story wants you counting.

Killing Forgemaw also opens the dial. Covenant Escalation can be set from the hub after this, from a story-quiet zero up to ten. Nothing in the mines changes its lines because you turned the dial. Forgemaw does not know what tier you brought. See the end of this document. The difficulty is not a plot.

## Act IV — The Sunken Ruins

Below the slag, the Fracture opens into flooded halls. Coral. Air pockets. Cult murals of a choir that sang the sea open. A drowned spire shows through every vista, and the light inside it is Breach-violet. This is the Crimson Sea's ruin, pulled under Elyria and left on its side. It is not a story about the crater falling in on the town. The town is still there. The tree is still the tree.

Breath is the law of the place. The water spends it. When the breath is gone, the water spends health. Each floor has an air bell. Drowned cultists hunt anything that stays at the surface too long. The floors the game gives you are the Gallery, the Pocket, the Murals, the Spire, and the Nave.

The Drowned Choir waits in the nave. In v0.9.0 it is one body. It fights you above the water, then it goes under, and the second phase is the water rising. **Open** whether the Choir should be many singers using one will. The outline describes a multi-body thing. The build is the single body. Either reading can be true later. Do not write a hymn that depends on the headcount until that is chosen.

When the Choir breaks, the temple drains just enough. In the nave is a coral sarcophagus, cracked from within. It is empty. The pad beside it is the way home.

**Open.** The approved outline has more than the build. The cult wrote Joe into their hymns as the dry prophet who promised a god that would never drown. He traded them a Breach seed for passage and sealed the exits behind him. Hymn-shards were going to tell that at the air bells. None of those lines or items are in v0.9.0. The cult has no name. What was in the sarcophagus, if anything, is the question the empty shell is for. The answer is not in this storyline.

**Proposed.** Joe believed the seed was his gift and his fare. The cult believed the god would be dry-shod. Both were hiring language for a thing that was already walking up out of its own castoffs, using Joe's deals as doors. That is a reading of the shells. It is not an identification of the walker. Leave the walker unnamed.

## Act V — The Inverted Spire

**Open.** Not in the game. This is the outline ShobieShy approved on 5 Nov 2026.

The shaft becomes sky. Platforms from the Shattered Peaks hang in a vertical void on wind and Breach crystal, with long falls between them. Harpies and storm elementals try to push you off. Wind corridors reverse while you are still using them. The landmark is the Inverted Spire, a tower growing downward from a false sky. Every act you have survived is cut into its facets. This is the picture of the Fracture: five dungeons, one throat.

The story tightens on Joe and the Demon Lord. Echoes of Bob's research, of the Association's refusal, and of Joe's voice in the wind add up to a uglier errand than sabotage. Joe was not only passing through. He was carving a road for something that needed five biomes' worth of death to wake. Side ledges are the last clean lore before the Core: who he was before the Breach, and why the Demon Lord let him walk this deep. Those pages are not written.

The Storm Warden keeps the spire's wind locks and fights you across platforms that fail. There is no safe floor. When the Warden falls, the heart of the spire opens into a crystal throat toward the Core. The boss pad will still let you waystone home. If you look down the throat you do not find a fourth empty cocoon. You find a lit path. Joe is no longer ahead of you. He is waiting.

## The Core

**Open.** Not outlined fight by fight, and not built. The order is decided.

You fight Crazy Joe. He is corrupted, and his spells feel like yours, because he has spent the descent learning the shaft by walking it the way a Summoned does, one brand at a time. Then you fight the Demon Lord. Then the Demon Lord escapes. That escape is the end of the main campaign and the reason DLC has somewhere to point. His phases, his loot, and the shot of him leaving are all unwritten.

The Association head, nameless on purpose so far, has been terrified of him the whole time and has spent nothing that would have put an army in this throat. You were the affordable option. Joe was the accident they banished. Bob was the man in the tree they assigned you to, hoping the problem would stay at the edge of town.

**Proposed.** When the Demon Lord goes, the ward-pillars on Eldoria's square flicker violet for one breath. Nobody in the Flagon agrees on the color. The next town you are sent to has already heard a rumor of that flicker. That is a DLC hook, not a scene in v0.9.0.

## What the shells are for

Three empties, in order, all visible by the end of Act IV: the cocoon, the slag-cast body, the coral sarcophagus. The approved line is that something is climbing toward the Core and shedding skins as it goes. Act V, if it ships as outlined, refuses you a fourth shell and gives you Joe instead, waiting.

Those are different images, and they should stay different until someone decides they are the same. Joe-as-the-thing-in-the-cocoon is not the current outline. Joe-as-the-man-who-cut-the-road is.

## After the story, the same story

Once a labyrinth's boss is dead, you can run that labyrinth again. While you are still finishing that replay, it is called the Abyss. When you beat the replay, the last floor stops being the last floor. That is Endless Descent. The new floors are generated. The story floors stay as they were built. You keep going until you can't, or until you don't want to.

Covenant Escalation is the dial on that loop, tiers from a quiet zero up to ten. Harder enemies, better loot, the same sentences. It does not add a chapter, a confession, or a different ending. Turning it does not make Bob say the Demon Lord's name. The omens it rolls — Ember Rain, a blood moon, wards that refuse to stay down — are weather on the fight, not plot.

Gear from deep in that descent can, rarely, show up in an ordinary story run, scaled to the floor you are actually on. **Open.** The rate is not tuned, so it does not happen yet. Each co-op player would roll their own.

**The Forgotten Abyss** is the name reserved for the endless run after Act V and the Core. It is not this loop. **Open** whether three names for "down, but more" is too many. Until that is decided, the Abyss is the replay, Endless Descent is the infinite version of that replay, and the Forgotten Abyss is the one you have not been given.

## DLC, as a door and not a plot

**Open.** Each later labyrinth gets its own town and its own Mage Association chapter, with shops, training, and lore, the way an adventurer's guild plants a hall. The main game keeps Eldoria. DLC has level minimums, and the numbers are not chosen. Covenant Escalation on those labyrinths waits until the main game is beaten, and it stays a dial there too. The Demon Lord's escape is the narrative excuse to send you. The towns themselves have no names yet.

## Quests, as the story actually hands them to you

You are pushed, more than hired.

A New Home is the permit and the walk. Trash Talk is three gnawers, the Filth, and Joe's trash. Report to Bob ends the tutorial and starts the crystal. The Breach points you at the crater. The Overgrown Deep is the Matriarch, with Joe's Marks beside it. The Slagworks is Forgemaw, with Joe's Seals beside it. Nothing in the quest log yet asks you to silence the Choir, climb the spire, or name the Demon Lord.

The bounty board after Forgemaw is work, not lore: gnawers, heaps, spore husks, paid in gold, repeatable forever. It does not advance the Fracture.

## Proposed

- Bob already knows the Demon Lord's name and will not spend it early.
- The crystal's "underwhelming" is the Demon Lord's block, the same block that ruined Bob's rites. You got through because Joe was sloppy enough to be unstoppable.
- The Clerk's "Old Oak" is a survey. Bob's "Great Oak" is the name he uses when he cares, which is rare.
- Joe thought the Breach seed was his bargain. The cult thought they were buying a dry god. Both were opening doors for whatever is shedding the shells. The walker stays unnamed.
- The Demon Lord's exit flickers the ward-pillars over Eldoria for one breath, and the next town hears about it before you arrive.
- The prism is an Association focus cracked in the rite. Seal it and the dome steadies. Crack it and you are stronger and the crater is wider. He has been watching through it either way, so the choice introduces you.
