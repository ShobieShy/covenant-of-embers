## Act I — The Goblin Warrens

After the Breach crater fight, the Labyrinth gate opens into packed-earth tunnels shored with stolen timber and goblin banners. These are the raiding dens that once spilled out toward Willowbrook: ambush rooms, spike traps, and gnawer packs nesting in the dark. The deeper the party goes, the clearer it becomes that the goblins are not ruling this shaft. They are fleeing something below, and their crude wards against "green fire" mark tunnels that lead down, not out. Bob's research notes and Association scouts name a war-band led by a crowned brute who taxes every raid. On floor 5 the party finds his throne hall, a handmade arena of bone piles and stolen Association crates. The **Goblin King** fights with slamming, throwing, and a roar that calls in the last of his pack. When he dies, the crown falls as a boss drop, and a waystone lights for the trip home to Town Square. Clearing the Warrens does not seal the Fracture. The crater portal now also offers a second descent: roots and fungus breaking through the floorboards of the lowest warren. Joe's graffiti starts here in chalk and blood, pointing deeper. The Act ends with the party holding a goblin key-token and a clear choice: return to the hub, or push into the green.

## Act II — The Overgrown Deep (The Blightcoil)

Below the Warrens the Labyrinth becomes a jungle that should not exist underground: one walkable circular cavern spiraling around the **Rotheart**, a dying world-tree corrupted by the Breach. The **Sump** is poison mire at the bottom. The **Canopy Ring** is root bridges and moss ledges. The **Crown** is fungal bloom and drifting spore fog. The goblins who fled here are husks with spore-caps, and the **Mossbound** treant wardens now strangle anything that comes close. Crazy Joe came through first. His ritual marks are carved into the bark, and he has been feeding the Rotheart Breach-fire to speed the rot. The **Verdant Mother**, a dryad bound to the tree, waits on the Canopy and begs the party to burn out the infection before it climbs higher. Collecting three of Joe's marks proves how far the sabotage has gone, and the Canopy shrine is the last clean breath in the cavern. At the Crown the party faces the **Blightmaw Matriarch**, a spore beast fused to the tree's corrupted heart, in three phases with no locked arena. When she falls, the Rotheart dies, the fog clears, and a door opens toward the Slagworks. Beneath her sits an empty cocoon, torn open from the inside: something the Verdant Mother never mentioned, and a hook that points past Act II.

## Act III — The Slagworks

The Slagworks door drops the party into Ironforge's buried mines: rails, ore veins, and lava channels under a cavern so hot the air shimmers. Association charts called this a collapse. The truth is that Joe flooded the lower shafts with Breach-fire, and the furnaces never went cold. Heat zones force the party onto gantries and overlooks, while mine carts and constructs still run on half-broken schedules. The landmark of every floor is the great **furnace-anvil**, a molten mouth that orients every arena. The remaining workers are not miners. They are trolls and forge-constructs bound by Joe's brands, hammering Breach-ore into weapons for something deeper. A surviving foreman or scrap-spirit can name the smith who kept the fires lit after the flood: **Forgemaw**, a troll who treats the party as uninvited ore. Loot here leans into heat and metal, and Joe's marks shift from bark carvings to slag seals burned into the rails. Forgemaw's boss floor is a crucible arena with lava strips, a gantry ring for flanks, and phases that break cover and force the party through the heat. When he falls, the main lift unlocks and the waystone home lights. At the bottom of the shaft, cooling slag has cast a perfect negative of a humanoid figure that is no longer there: another empty shell, and the first clear sign that whatever tore free of the Blightcoil cocoon walked this way.

## Act IV — The Sunken Ruins

0.9.0 ships this outline. It is not Breach fallout. Old Oak is not renamed.

Below the Slagworks, the Fracture opens into flooded halls taken from Crimson Sea ruins: coral-choked galleries, air pockets, and cult murals of a choir that sang the sea open. Breath becomes the Act's leash. Rising water, crush zones, and long swims reuse the Breath meter, while sea horrors and drowned cultists hunt anything that surfaces too long. The landmark is a drowned temple spire visible through every flooded vista, still glowing with Breach-violet from the inside. Joe's trail here is liturgical. The cultists wrote his name into their hymns as the "dry prophet" who promised them a god that would never drown. Collectible hymn-shards and pressure-locked vaults tell how he traded them a Breach seed for passage, then sealed the exits behind him. Optional air-bell shrines let the party catch their breath and piece the story together without soft-locking co-op. The Act boss is the **Drowned Choir**, a multi-body entity that phases between submerged and surfaced forms and uses the water level as a weapon. When the Choir breaks, the temple drains just enough to open a vertical shaft of wind and broken masonry. In the drained nave lies a third empty vessel: a coral sarcophagus cracked from within. Whatever is climbing toward the Core is shedding skins as it goes.

## Act V — The Inverted Spire

The shaft becomes sky. Platforms from the Shattered Peaks hang in a vertical void, held by wind and Breach crystal, with long falls between them. Harpies and storm elementals push the party off ledges, and wind corridors reverse mid-fight. The landmark is the **Inverted Spire** itself, a tower growing downward from a false sky, every Act's geometry fused into its facets. This is the Fracture made visible: the five dungeons collapsed into one throat. Story beats tighten around Joe and the Demon Lord. Echoes of Bob's research, Association failure, and Joe's voice in the wind make clear that Joe did not merely pass through. He was carving a road for something that needed five biomes' worth of death to wake. Side ledges hold the last clean lore before the Core: who Joe was before the Breach, and why the Demon Lord let him walk freely this deep. The Act boss is the **Storm Warden**, keeper of the Spire's wind locks, fought across breaking platforms with no safe floor. When the Warden falls, the Spire's heart opens into a crystal throat that leads toward the Core. The party can waystone home one last time from the boss pad. Looking down the throat, they see not another empty cocoon, but a lit path: Joe is no longer ahead of them. He is waiting.

## After the final act

Decided 2026-11-11. This does not replace the Act V ending above. After beating a labyrinth, you can replay the same labyrinth over and over. Once you beat it, its final level is no longer the final level; there is no final level. You keep descending with increasing difficulty, loot drops, and rewards until you can't go on. This is the endgame loop. It applies to the main Breach labyrinth and to each DLC labyrinth after its story is cleared. Story acts stay handmade. Post-clear floors are randomly generated with escalating stats.

The mode is called "the Abyss" while you are progressing through it (the finite version), and once you beat it, it becomes "Endless Descent" (the infinite post-clear version). Covenant Escalation stays purely mechanical, same story. It is the one difficulty dial (the old separate names Chaos mode and Mayhem tiers are that same dial). It turns with depth in Endless Descent.

Gear earned going deep in Endless Descent, under Covenant Escalation, can appear as super rare drops in normal base-story labyrinth runs. The exact drop rate is unspecified for now, to be tuned later. It should be rare enough to be a genuine surprise and not break story balance. The deeper the Covenant Escalation tier the item came from, the crazier it is and the rarer it is as a base-game drop. Whether those drops are level-scaled down for story runs, and whether co-op shares the roll, is open.

In code the dial is still `scripts/loot/vigil.gd` (`Vigil`, tiers 1–10) and `scripts/labyrinth/labyrinth_state.gd` `remix()`. The class is not renamed. Whether the player can also set it by hand, whether these names clash with The Forgotten Abyss in [PLAN_NEXT.md](PLAN_NEXT.md), and whether "the Abyss" means each labyrinth's descent generally, stays open. The Forgotten Abyss name is not changed here.
